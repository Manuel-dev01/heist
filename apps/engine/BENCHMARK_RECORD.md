# Saved benchmark verification

verifyBenchmarkResult({ plan, report, entries }) is a pure offline verifier. It returns a boolean, a value-free reason code on failure, and the number of entries checked. It performs no file, provider, wallet or network operation.

The verifier revalidates the complete materialized plan and corpus, the report's canonical hash, strict current attempt records and metadata, and the exact observed prefix of the seeded paired schedule. Task, system-prompt and configuration hashes, policy, all plan-derived metadata, ordered references and metadata hashes must agree. It recomputes metrics from the records rather than trusting the saved summary and requires no aggregation exclusions.

Counts, whole-block reservations, dispatched-call bounds, request-byte allowances, reported-usage consistency and retained JSON entry byte sizes are checked against the plan. A stopped run may retain an unfinished paired block and one final record beyond the soft evidence threshold. Provider-reported over-cap response counts are observational declarations, not independently proven per-response usage.

A successful result establishes local content integrity and internal consistency only. It does not prove provider provenance, real execution, oracle truth, independent trials, defense efficacy, actual billing, payment settlement or a blockchain anchor. A fabricated result can be rehashed to remain internally consistent. Preserve the original local artifacts and their source labels.

New reports may explicitly include reportedUsage.unobservedSettlements (admitted invocations minus known and observed-unknown responses) and missingUsageInvocations (admitted invocations minus known responses). The verifier requires exact nonnegative values when present and accepts legacy reports omitting either field. These counters expose missing observations; they do not establish provider-side cancellation, completed HTTP requests or zero billing.

When a plan explicitly includes maxConsecutiveErrors, verification scans the actual ordered entries and rejects any entry after the first reached error threshold, even if a later success resets the ending streak or a report claims another stop reason. Reaching the threshold at the final entry requires stopped status. A consecutive_error_limit claim requires the configured, reached threshold; legitimate preexisting provider/output/byte/evidence/persistence/abort/deadline reasons may retain precedence. Evidence crossings and observed output-overshoot precedence are checked where observable. Runs with the optional field absent retain legacy verification behavior and hashes. Rehashing an old continued run with a newly inserted threshold does not make that execution consistent.

At a reached error gate, a retained request_byte_limit cause must end in model_error and leave at least one recorded model round undispatched. A byte-admission denial occurs before dispatch; a resealed all-rounds-dispatched error burst cannot use that cause to replace the gate.
